
# Everyone Thought Someone Else Was Handling Cybersecurity

In many small businesses, cybersecurity falls into a dangerous gap.

The owner assumes the IT company handles it. The IT company manages the computers but assumes the business handles employee access and policies. The office manager manages passwords. The bookkeeper handles payroll. Employees report suspicious emails to whoever seems available.

Everyone is handling part of security, but **no one clearly owns it**.

That becomes a serious problem when an employee leaves, a device is lost, an email account is compromised, or someone needs to decide what happens next.

Some businesses are also required to maintain written security policies. Tax professionals need a Written Information Security Plan, or WISP. Certain financial businesses must maintain a written information security program, and healthcare organizations must document their security policies and procedures.

Even when a formal plan is not required, every business needs one person responsible for coordinating security.

## One Simple Goal

> [!TIP]
> Before the end of the week, **choose one person** to be the business’s cybersecurity point of contact.

*They do not have to fix every technical problem.* They only need to know:

1. Who manages email, devices, passwords, payroll, and backups.
2. Who approves and removes employee access.
3. Who to contact during a security incident.
4. Where security procedures and vendor information are stored.

Write those answers on one page and make sure the owner and one backup person can access it.

An outside IT provider can manage the technology, but the business still needs someone internally who knows **who is responsible for what**.

Cybersecurity problems become much harder to solve when everyone thought someone else was handling them.
