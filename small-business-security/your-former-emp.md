
# Your Former Employee May Still Have the Keys

When an employee leaves, most small businesses remember the obvious things: collect the laptop, get the office key back, and remove them from the schedule.

*But digital access is easier to forget.*

A former employee may still be able to reach company email, shared files, payroll systems, password managers, vendor portals, banking tools, or cloud accounts long after their last day.

`That does not always mean they plan to misuse it. But access that is no longer needed is still a security risk.`

## One Simple Goal

> [!IMPORTANT]
>Create a one-page offboarding checklist and use it every time someone leaves.

At minimum:
- Disable their email and cloud accounts.
- Remove access to payroll, banking, shared drives, and password managers.
- Recover company devices, keys, and security tokens.
- Change any shared passwords they knew.
- Preserve business files and email before deleting anything.

Do not assume the IT company, office manager, or payroll provider handled every system.

Termination is not complete until the person’s access is removed everywhere they had it.

A clean offboarding process protects the business, the remaining employees, and even the former employee from being blamed if an old account is compromised later.
