# Stop Memorizing Passwords: Use a Password Manager

The advice to use a strong, unique password for every account is correct.

It is also unrealistic to expect someone to memorize dozens of them.

That is why people fall into familiar habits:

- Reusing the same password
- Making small variations of one password
- Saving passwords in notes or messages
- Choosing passwords that are easy to remember and easy to guess
- Resetting the same accounts over and over

The problem is not that you need a better memory.

The problem is that your memory should not be responsible for protecting every account you own.

## Let a Password Manager Do the Remembering

A password manager stores your passwords in a protected vault. It can also generate long, random passwords and fill them in when you need them.

Instead of memorizing a different password for every account, you protect the vault with one strong master passphrase.

That master passphrase should:

- Be long
- Be unique
- Never be used on another account
- Be memorable to you but difficult for someone else to guess

Turn on multifactor authentication for your password manager and securely save any recovery information it provides.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Do not store your only recovery method somewhere that you cannot access without first opening the password manager.

## Start With Five Accounts

Do not try to replace every password in one night.

Start with the accounts that could create the most damage if someone gained access:

1. Your primary email account
2. Your bank or financial accounts
3. Your Apple, Google, or Microsoft account
4. Your phone carrier account
5. Your most important social-media account

Use the password manager to generate a different password for each one.

Your primary email should be near the top of the list because it is often used to reset the passwords for your other accounts.

## A Password Manager Is Not a Magic Shield

A password manager does not make you impossible to hack.

You still need to:

- Protect the master passphrase
- Enable MFA
- Keep your devices updated
- Avoid entering passwords after clicking suspicious links
- Review security alerts
- Maintain a tested recovery method

The goal is not perfect security.

The goal is to stop one weak, guessed, or reused password from exposing several parts of your life.

## One Simple Goal

> [!TIP]
> Choose a reputable password manager and replace the passwords on your five most important accounts with unique passwords this week.

You do not need to memorize more passwords.

You need a safer system for managing them.
